The tool page is [tamperbox.dev/dmarc-checker](https://tamperbox.dev/dmarc-checker). It opens on a live check of `tamperbox.dev`; replace that domain to inspect your own. This page is the long version: how the record is found and read, every rule applied to it, what each finding means, and how the builder and the path to enforcement write the next record.

<div class="tb-callout"><strong>Where processing happens:</strong> a domain is sent to the Tamperbox server, which asks public DNS for the TXT record at <code>_dmarc.&lt;domain&gt;</code>. A pasted record is parsed and graded in your browser, and every draft you build stays on the page. Nothing is stored, and copying a draft does not publish it.</div>

## What the tool answers

DMARC tells receivers what to do with mail that fails both aligned SPF and aligned DKIM, and where to send reports about it. The tool answers:

1. **Is there a valid record, and what does it say?** One record at the exact name, every tag explained.
2. **How much does it protect?** The policy, the percentage it applies to, and the subdomain policy.
3. **Will you hear about failures?** Aggregate report addresses, their format, and whether an outside receiver needs to authorise them.
4. **What is the next safe step?** A draft record for each step from monitoring to reject, with every change kept in History.

It does not read aggregate reports, so it cannot say whether your legitimate mail already passes.

## Inputs

### A domain

`example.com`. When you stop typing, or on Enter, the server reads the TXT records at `_dmarc.example.com` and keeps the ones that begin with `v=DMARC1`. Two or more is a finding; none shows a starting draft.

### A DMARC record

Paste the record itself, with or without quotes: `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com`. It is parsed in the browser and shown as "Pasted record". Publication, and the policy a parent domain may publish, are not checked.

### Files

Drop a `.txt` file anywhere on the page, or use Open file. It is read in the browser.

## Reading the result

### Verdict and chips

The verdict names the policy's effect: no policy, monitoring only, quarantining or rejecting a share or all of failing mail, or an invalid record, followed by where the record came from (published, pasted, or a draft). The chips show the policy with its percentage, whether reports are on, and whether you are looking at the published record, a pasted one, or a draft; each one is a button that scrolls to the section that explains it.

### Findings

Everything found, worst first; passes fold into one line. The `p=none` finding carries a button that moves the draft one step forward. The [finding reference](#finding-reference) lists them all.

### Take it further

Links that carry a derived value into another tool: the mail route of the report mailbox's domain, BIMI readiness for the domain once the policy enforces, and DNSSEC on the zone. Each opens the destination with the value already in its box; nothing is put in the URL.

### History and Heal it

History logs every change: a rollout step, a builder edit, a Break it case or a Heal it repair, each with the record it replaced. Revert undoes the newest change and returns the draft to the record before it; Reset input returns to the published or pasted record. Heal it offers the repairs the tool found: add `p=none`, correct a misspelt policy, remove obsolete `pct`, make `rua` a `mailto:` address, choose which value to keep when a tag is duplicated, move a misplaced version tag to the front.

### Path to enforcement

Four targets, each a complete record built from the current tags: Monitor (`p=none`), Test quarantine (`p=quarantine; t=y`), Quarantine all (`p=quarantine`), Reject (`p=reject`). The current step is marked "You are here". Choosing a target applies it as a draft. The test step requests one level less enforcement under [RFC 9989 section 4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7), so quarantine in test mode requests monitoring.

### Build the record

Policy, subdomain policy, percentage, aggregate and failure report addresses, DKIM and SPF alignment, and failure options. Every change applies at once and is graded like a published record.

### Publish this record

The draft as one TXT record, with the name to publish it at and a copy button. Copying does not publish.

### The record, tag by tag

Every tag with its label and meaning; unknown tags are marked as ignored by receivers.

## Method

Each rule below names the section of [RFC 9989](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989), the DMARC specification, that it comes from. RFC 9989 obsoletes RFC 7489, the Informational predecessor; where the tool still follows RFC 7489, the rule says so, and [Standards and conformance](#standards-and-conformance) lists every difference.

### Finding the record

The server asks public DNS, through one DNS-over-HTTPS resolver, for TXT records at exactly `_dmarc.<domain>` ([RFC 9989, section 4.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.5)). A record split into several strings is joined first: "the module performing DMARC evaluation MUST concatenate these strings" (section 4.5). Only records that begin with `v=DMARC1` are kept, as in step 2 of the [DNS tree walk, section 4.10](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10).

- Two or more records are discarded at that name and discovery continues toward a parent policy, following section 4.10. No first-record-wins policy is inferred.
- No record: the verdict is "No DMARC record" and a starting draft, `v=DMARC1; p=none`, is prepared for the builder.

The lookup moves up the tree, bounded to eight queries, and stops at an explicit `psd=n` or `psd=y` boundary. [Section 4.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10.1) has receivers walk from the Author Domain to its Organizational Domain and then to a public suffix domain when no record exists at the exact name. The selected source domain is displayed. Domain existence is not queried, so inherited policy is shown for existing subdomains and `np` remains a limitation.

### Version and tag syntax

The record is split on semicolons, and each part must be `name=value`, as in the formal definition of [section 4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8).

- The first tag must be exactly `v=DMARC1`, case-sensitive. [Section 4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7): "This tag MUST be the first tag in the list." If it is not, "the entire record MUST be ignored." The tool stops: "Not a DMARC record". Heal it can move a misplaced `v=DMARC1` to the front.
- A part without `=`: FAIL, "Not a tag: X".
- A tag that appears twice: FAIL, "name appears twice". DMARC uses the DKIM tag-list syntax (section 4.7), and [RFC 6376, section 3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6376#section-3.2) says "if a tag name does occur more than once, the entire tag-list is invalid." Identical duplicates can be removed; conflicting values get one repair per value ("Keep p=none", "Keep p=reject"). While duplicates remain, the policy is not graded and the builder, the path to enforcement and the record to publish are hidden.

### Tag values

- `p`, `sp` and `np` accept only `none`, `quarantine` or `reject` (the `dmarc-request` rule of section 4.8); `adkim` and `aspf` only `r` or `s` (`dmarc-rors`). Anything else: FAIL, "name=value is not valid".
- No `p`: INFO, "No p= policy: defaults to none". An otherwise valid record defaults to monitoring under [section 4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7).
- `pct` produces CHECK, "pct=X is obsolete". It never reduces enforcement; [RFC 9989 appendix A.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#appendix-A.6) removes percentage sampling.
- A tag outside `v`, `p`, `sp`, `np`, `t`, `psd`, `pct`, `rua`, `ruf`, `adkim`, `aspf`, `fo`, `rf`, `ri`: CHECK, "Unknown tag X". Section 4.7: "unknown tags MUST be ignored."

### Policy strength

The grade follows [section 4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7). Missing `p` defaults to `none`; `pct` is ignored. Test mode `t=y` reduces reject to quarantine and quarantine to none. The display reports a requested policy, not a guarantee of receiver handling.

| Record | Severity | Title | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `p=reject`, no test mode | PASS | p=reject | Section 4.7, `p` |
| `p=quarantine`, no test mode | PASS | p=quarantine | Section 4.7, `p` |
| `p=quarantine; t=y` | CHECK | DMARC test mode (t=y), p=none only monitors | Section 4.7, `t` |
| `p=reject; t=y` | CHECK | DMARC test mode (t=y), p=quarantine | Section 4.7, `t` |
| `p=none` | CHECK | p=none only monitors | [Section 3.2.12](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.12), Monitoring Mode |
| `sp=none` with an enforcing `p` | CHECK | sp=none on subdomains | Section 4.7, `sp` |
| `adkim=s` or `aspf=s` | INFO | Strict alignment | [Section 3.2.10.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.10.2) |

For `sp`, section 4.7 says: "If both the "sp" tag is absent and the "np" tag is either absent or not applicable, the policy specified by the "p" tag MUST be applied for subdomains." So `sp=none` beside an enforcing `p` opens every existing subdomain on purpose. The `p=none` and `sp=none` grades are Tamperbox recommendations: the specification allows both.

### Report addresses

- No `rua`: CHECK, "No rua reports". Section 4.7: "If the tag is not provided, Mail Receivers MUST NOT generate aggregate feedback reports for the domain." Reports are optional in the specification; the CHECK is a Tamperbox recommendation, since [section 5.1.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-5.1.5) has the Domain Owner collect and analyse them before enforcing.
- Each comma-separated `rua` entry ([section 4.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.6)) must be a `mailto:` URI with one `@`, optionally followed by a size limit such as `!10m`. Anything else: FAIL, "Invalid report address X". This is stricter than the specification, where "Any valid URI can be specified" and "URIs involving schemes not supported by Mail Receivers MUST be ignored" (section 4.7). The size limit is obsolete syntax that reporters ignore (section 4.8).
- An address on a domain that is neither the checked domain nor one of its subdomains: INFO, "Reports go to X", naming the record the receiving domain must publish, `<domain>._report._dmarc.<receiving-domain>`. [RFC 9990, section 4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990#section-4) says that when the Organizational Domains differ, "the following verification steps MUST be taken". That record is not looked up. For a pasted record the domain is unknown, so no note is made.
- Otherwise: PASS, "N report addresses".

`ruf`, `fo`, `rf` and `ri` are explained but not validated.

### The builder and the drafts

The builder writes tags in a fixed order (`v`, `p`, `sp`, `np`, `t`, `psd`, `pct`, `rua`, `ruf`, `adkim`, `aspf`, `fo`, `rf`, `ri`), keeps unknown tags at the end, and leaves out tags that only repeat a default (`pct=100`, `adkim=r`, `aspf=r`, `fo=0`, `rf=afrf`, `ri=86400`). The `adkim`, `aspf` and `fo` defaults are those of [section 4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7); the `pct`, `rf` and `ri` defaults are RFC 7489's. Starting with `v=DMARC1` keeps every draft valid under section 4.7.

A draft is graded by the same rules as a published record. The problems of the published record (duplicates, multiple records) are not carried into the draft's findings, except the multiple-records finding, which the draft cannot fix.

## Standards and conformance

The tool reads one DMARC record and grades its text. It is not a mail receiver: it evaluates no message, so SPF and DKIM results and identifier alignment are never computed. This section lists, per specification, what the tool implements, where it stops and where it is stricter or more lenient than the text.

DMARC was published as [RFC 7489](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7489) (Informational, 2015). In May 2026 three Standards Track documents obsoleted it: [RFC 9989](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989) for the mechanism (which also obsoletes RFC 9091, the experimental public suffix domain policy), [RFC 9990](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990) for aggregate reports and [RFC 9991](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9991) for failure reports, which also updates RFC 6591. The tool's tag table still follows RFC 7489 in several places, listed below.

### RFC 9989: finding the record

| Section | Requirement | What the tool does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [4.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.5) | Records are TXT at `_dmarc.<domain>`; strings MUST be concatenated | Looked up on the Tamperbox server through one public resolver; strings joined |
| [4.10](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10), step 2 | Records not starting with `v=DMARC1` are discarded; several records are all discarded | Same filter; multiple records at a name are discarded |
| [4.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10.1) | Without a record at the Author Domain, walk to the Organizational Domain and the public suffix domain | Implemented with at most eight queries |
| [4.10](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10) | The tree walk replaces the Public Suffix List and stops at a record with `psd=y` or `psd=n` | Implemented; source domain is shown |
| [3.2.13](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.13) | A non-existent domain is one answering NXDOMAIN; `np` applies to those | Not checked; `np` is only validated |
| [5.3.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-5.3.1) | A U-label Author Domain MUST be converted to an A-label | The domain is converted to A-labels before the lookup |

The lookup accepts `v` or `V` as the version tag name but requires the case-sensitive value `DMARC1`, following [section 4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8).

### RFC 9989: record format and tag values

All parsing happens in the browser, from the text the server returned or the text you pasted.

| Section | Requirement | What the tool does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7), `v` | First tag, value exactly `DMARC1`, else ignore the record | Same: the record is not read |
| [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) | Unknown tags MUST be ignored | CHECK finding, record still graded |
| [4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8) | Syntax errors MUST be discarded in favour of defaults or ignored | Stricter: a malformed part or an invalid value is a FAIL and makes the verdict invalid |
| [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7), `p` | Missing `p` is treated as `p=none` | Defaults to monitoring with an informational finding |
| [4.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10.1) | Invalid `p`, `sp` or `np` with a valid `rua`: act as `p=none`; otherwise no DMARC processing | Not modelled; the tool reports the record as invalid |
| [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7), `adkim`, `aspf` | `r` or `s`, default `r` | Validated; default left out of drafts |
| [4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8), `fo` | `0` or `1` with `d` and `s`, colon-separated; ignored without `ruf` | Explained, not validated |
| [4.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.6), `rua` and `ruf` | Comma-separated URIs; any valid URI | `rua` must be `mailto:`; `ruf` is not checked |
| [4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8) | `!size` suffix is obsolete; reporters ignore it | Accepted without comment |

### RFC 9989: tags removed and added since RFC 7489

[Appendix C.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#appendix-C.5) lists the tag changes. Under RFC 9989 a removed tag is simply an unknown tag, so receivers ignore it.

| Tag | Change in RFC 9989 | What the tool does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pct` | Removed ([appendix C.5.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#appendix-C.5.2), [A.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#appendix-A.6)) | Warned as obsolete and ignored for grading; no percentage rollout step |
| `rf` | Removed | Known tag with an explanation; not flagged |
| `ri` | Removed | Known tag with an explanation; not flagged |
| `t` | Added: `y` asks receivers to apply one level below the policy; default `n` | Validated and used to downgrade the effective policy |
| `np` | Added, imported from RFC 9091 | Known; values validated; not graded |
| `psd` | Added: `y`, `n` or `u`, default `u` | Validated and used to stop discovery |

Appendix A.6 explains the replacement: the `t` values `y` and `n` are "meant to be analogous" to `pct=0` and `pct=100`. A receiver that follows RFC 9989 treats `p=quarantine; pct=25` as full quarantine. Receivers still built on RFC 7489 may apply the percentage, which may differ from the current-policy grade shown here.

### RFC 9989: enforcement guidance

[Section 7.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-7.4) says "domains that host users who might post messages to mailing lists SHOULD NOT publish Domain Owner Assessment Policies of "p=reject"", and domains that publish `p=reject` "MUST apply valid DKIM signatures to their messages." The tool grades `p=reject` as PASS and offers Reject as the last step of the path to enforcement without this caveat. It cannot know who sends from the domain, so weigh section 7.4 yourself before that step.

[Section 5.1.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-5.1.7) ties the move to enforcement to "many months of consuming DMARC aggregate reports" in some cases. The tool does not read reports, so it cannot judge readiness.

### RFC 9990 and RFC 9991: reports

| Section | Requirement | What the tool does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [RFC 9990, 3.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990#section-3.5) | Malformed URIs SHOULD be ignored; deliver to every remaining URI | Flags the first malformed `rua` entry as a FAIL |
| [RFC 9990, 4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990#section-4) | When the policy's Organizational Domain differs from the destination's, query `<policy-domain>._report._dmarc.<destination-host>` | Names that record for the first external address; does not look it up |
| [RFC 9990, 4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990#section-4) | The comparison is between Organizational Domains | Approximated: an address outside the checked domain and its subdomains counts as external |
| [RFC 9991, 5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9991#section-5) | The same verification applies to `ruf` | Not done for `ruf` |
| [RFC 9991, 2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9991#section-2) | Failure report options come from `ruf` and `fo` | Explained only |

The approximation errs both ways: a report address at the parent domain of a checked subdomain is called external although it shares the Organizational Domain, and no external note is made for a pasted record.

### Alignment, SPF and DKIM

DMARC passes when SPF ([RFC 7208](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208)) or DKIM ([RFC 6376](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6376)) produces an authenticated identifier aligned with the From domain ([RFC 9989, section 4.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.4)). The tool only reads the `adkim` and `aspf` modes and explains the difference between relaxed and strict alignment ([sections 3.2.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.10.1) and [3.2.10.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.10.2)). It checks no SPF record, no DKIM key and no message; use the SPF and DKIM checkers for those.

### DNS names and internationalized domains

The `_dmarc` label is registered in the "Underscored and Globally Scoped DNS Node Names" registry of [RFC 8552](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8552); RFC 9989 [section 9.5](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-9.5) now holds that registration, and [RFC 8553](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8553) updated RFC 7489 to use the registry. [RFC 8616, section 6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8616#section-6) updated RFC 7489 so that U-labels are converted to A-labels and `rua` and `ruf` hold conventional addresses; RFC 9989 section 5.3.1 carries the A-label rule. The tool converts the domain you enter to A-labels before the lookup and accepts any non-space characters around the `@` of a report address.

## Finding reference

Each finding the checker can emit, grouped by what it judges, with what it means for mail sent as the domain. The RFC section column names the rule behind the finding; "none" marks a Tamperbox recommendation that the specification does not require.

### Record and tag syntax

Problems that make receivers ignore the record or parts of it. Links point to RFC 9989 unless marked.

| Finding | Severity | What it means for mail | Fix | RFC section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No DMARC record (verdict) | FAIL | No policy at this name; a parent policy may apply | Publish the starting draft | [4.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10.1) |
| N DMARC records | FAIL | Receivers discard every record at the name | Delete all but one | [4.10](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10) |
| Not a DMARC record (verdict) | FAIL | Receivers ignore the whole record | Put v=DMARC1 first | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |
| Not a tag: X | FAIL | Receivers discard the part | Write name=value | [4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8) |
| name appears twice | FAIL | The tag list is invalid; the policy is not graded | Keep one value | [RFC 6376, 3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6376#section-3.2) |
| name=value is not valid | FAIL | Bad `p`, `sp` or `np`: `p=none` if `rua` is valid, else no DMARC; bad `adkim` or `aspf`: default `r` | Use a valid value | [4.10.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.10.1), [4.8](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.8) |
| No p= policy: defaults to none | INFO | Receivers act as if `p=none` | Add p=none | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |
| pct=X is obsolete | CHECK | Current receivers ignore percentage sampling | Remove pct | [A.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#appendix-A.6) |
| Invalid report address X | FAIL | Receivers skip that address | Use mailto: | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |
| Unknown tag X | CHECK | Receivers ignore it | Review or remove it | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |

### Policy strength

How much of the failing mail the policy acts on.

| Finding | Severity | What it means for mail | Fix | RFC section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p=none only monitors | CHECK | Spoofed mail is still delivered | Move to quarantine when reports are clean | none (Tamperbox recommendation); [3.2.12](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.12) defines the mode |
| DMARC test mode (t=y) | CHECK | Effective policy is one level below the declared policy | Review reports before disabling test mode | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |
| sp=none on subdomains | CHECK | Subdomains can be spoofed | Remove sp or match p | none (Tamperbox recommendation); [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) defines `sp` |
| p=reject, p=quarantine | PASS | Failing mail is refused or quarantined | | [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) |

### Reports and alignment

Who receives aggregate reports and how strictly identities must match.

| Finding | Severity | What it means for mail | Fix | RFC section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No rua reports | CHECK | You cannot see who sends as the domain | Add rua=mailto:… | none (Tamperbox recommendation); [4.7](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.7) makes `rua` optional |
| Reports go to X | INFO | The receiving domain must authorise the reports | Publish the authorisation record there | [RFC 9990, 4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9990#section-4) |
| Strict alignment | INFO | Subdomain-signed or subdomain-bounced mail fails | Relaxed is usually enough | [3.2.10.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-3.2.10.2) |
| N report addresses | PASS | Aggregate reports are on | | [4.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989#section-4.6) |

## Guide

### From p=none to reject

1. Make sure `rua` points at a mailbox or a reporting service you read. Raw aggregate reports are XML; a parsing service makes them readable.
2. Read a few weeks of reports. List every source that sends as the domain: your mail host, newsletter tool, ticketing, invoicing, and anything you forgot.
3. Make each one pass SPF or DKIM with a domain aligned with the From domain. Check their records with the SPF and DKIM checkers.
4. Choose Test quarantine, review the draft `p=quarantine; t=y`, and publish it only after checking receiver compatibility. Current receivers treat this as monitoring. Remove test mode to request quarantine after reviewing legitimate mail.
5. When the reports stay clean, move to Quarantine all, then Reject, the same way.

### No DMARC record

Publish the starting draft shown on the page, `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@<domain>`, at `_dmarc.<domain>` as a TXT record. Then follow the rollout above.

### Two records at the _dmarc name

A provider migration usually leaves the old record beside the new one. Delete every record but one, then check the domain again.

### A misspelt policy, a missing p, a bad percentage

Heal it offers the exact repair: the closest valid policy for a misspelling, `p=none` for a missing policy, removing obsolete `pct`. Each repair is a draft; copy and publish it.

### Report addresses

Write each address as `mailto:name@domain`; separate several with commas. If the reports go to a domain other than yours (a reporting service), that domain must publish `<yourdomain>._report._dmarc.<their-domain>`; the service's documentation says what to publish, and the INFO finding names the record.

### Subdomains

Leave `sp` unset so subdomains follow `p`, or set it to the same policy. `sp=none` on an enforcing domain leaves every subdomain open to spoofing.

### Break it cases

Each case is built from the record you entered or looked up.

| Case | What it does to your record | Heal it offers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No policy | Removes `p=` | Add p=none |
| Policy typo | Misspells the policy (`rejected`, `quarantined`, `nnone`) | Change p=… to the valid value |
| Version not first | Moves `v=DMARC1` after the first tag | Put corrected v=DMARC1 first |
| Bad report address | Strips `mailto:` from rua | Make rua a mailto: address |
| Invalid test mode | Sets `t=invalid` | Disable invalid test mode |
| Duplicate tag | Adds a second, conflicting `p=` | Keep p=none, Keep p=reject |

## Worked example

The page opens on a live check of `tamperbox.dev`. Its published records and findings can change over time.

For the deterministic walkthrough below, enter `harbor-supply.example`. This reserved `.example` domain is answered from synthetic data in your browser; it sends no live lookup. Its record:

```text
v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@harbor-supply.example; ruf=mailto:forensics@harbor-supply.example; fo=1
```

**Monitoring only: no DMARC enforcement requested.** The findings are "p=none only monitors" (CHECK, with a button to the next step) and "1 report address" (PASS). Choose Test quarantine: the draft becomes `v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; t=y; rua=…; ruf=…; fo=1`, History logs the change with Revert, and the record to publish shows the draft at `_dmarc.harbor-supply.example`. Replace the sample with your own domain and every section, card and link is rewritten for it.

## For agents

The Tamperbox MCP server runs the same checks. Search its catalog for the DMARC operation, inspect the input schema, and call it with the domain or the record. Keep the warning block with any conclusion:

- The tool grades the policy text; whether legitimate mail passes is in the aggregate reports.
- Parent policy is discovered through a bounded DNS tree walk; inherited `np` applicability is not verified because domain existence is not checked.
- External report authorisation records are named, not looked up.
- The `pct` tag is obsolete and does not reduce the effective policy. The `t` tag controls test mode.

See [Connect an MCP client](/mcp/connect/) and [Code mode](/mcp/code-mode/) for the calling pattern, and [Security and privacy](/reference/security/) for where the domain travels.

## Limits

- The policy, not the reports: enforcement readiness is not assessed.
- Parent policy is discovered through a bounded DNS tree walk; inherited `np` applicability is not verified because domain existence is not checked.
- `ruf`, `fo`, `rf` and `ri` are explained, not validated.
- The tag table recognizes `t` and `psd`; legacy `rf` and `ri` explanations remain and are not flagged as removed.
- One DNS answer, from one public resolver, at one moment.
- Nothing is stored; copying a draft does not publish it.
