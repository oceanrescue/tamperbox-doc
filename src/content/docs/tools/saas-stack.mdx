The tool page is [tamperbox.dev/saas-stack](https://tamperbox.dev/saas-stack). It opens on `harbor-supply.example`, a sample domain on the reserved `.example` zone whose TXT records hold tokens for six services, Google twice, and one token the catalog does not know. This page is the long version: what the finder reads, every rule it applies, what each finding means and how to act on it safely.

<div class="tb-callout"><strong>Where processing happens:</strong> a domain is sent to the Tamperbox server, which asks a public DNS resolver for the TXT records at that name. Matching the records against the catalog runs in your browser. Pasted records never leave the browser. The sample is answered in the browser. Nothing is stored.</div>

## What the tool answers

1. **Which services has this domain been connected to?** Every TXT record that starts with a service's verification prefix.
2. **What are they for?** Each service grouped by purpose: identity and productivity, engineering, communication, payments, sales and legal, marketing, email, security, privacy, certificates.
3. **Who sends mail as the domain?** The services named by the includes of its SPF record.
4. **Is anything unexplained?** Two tokens for one service, and records that look like tokens for services not in the catalog.

It does not log in anywhere, does not check whether an account exists, and does not read records on subdomains.

## Inputs

### A domain

`example.com`, or a full address or an email address pasted into the box, which becomes the domain. The lookup runs when you stop typing or on Enter. Only the name you enter is read: `_github-challenge-example.example.com` style records on underscored names are not seen unless you enter that name.

### Pasted records

Paste values as they are (`google-site-verification=…`), quoted TXT strings, or answer lines from `dig` or `nslookup` (`example.com. 300 IN TXT "MS=ms12345678"`, `example.com text = "…"`). A value split into several quoted strings is joined, as resolvers do. Text with `=`, `:`, quotes or more than one line is read as records; a bare name is looked up.

### Files

A `.txt` file dropped on the page or opened with Add a file is read in the browser.

## Reading the result

### Verdict and chips

"6 services suggested on example.com: Google, Microsoft 365, Atlassian, Stripe and 2 more." The verdict is a check when a service has more than one token. Other verdicts: "No TXT records", "No verification tokens. The SPF record names N mail senders.", and "No known services". Chips count the services, the duplicates, the mail senders and the records not in the catalog.

### Findings, Take it further, History

Findings worst first, passes folded. Take it further hands the SPF record itself to the [SPF Checker](https://tamperbox.dev/spf-checker), which counts its lookups, and, when the record names senders, the domain to the [DMARC Checker](https://tamperbox.dev/dmarc-checker) and the [DKIM Record Checker](https://tamperbox.dev/dkim-checker). History logs every Break it case with Revert and Reset input.

### Services

Grouped by purpose, each with what the token proves ("Search Console or Workspace ownership") and the token, with the variable part marked. A service with two or more tokens carries a Check badge.

### Sends mail through

Each SPF include, named when it matches a known sender, otherwise shown as not in the catalog.

### Not in the catalog

Records that look like verification tokens for unknown services.

### Every TXT record

Every record with its kind (Token, SPF, Unknown, Other) and what it means. Closed by default; Copy result includes it.

## Method

### 1. Reading TXT records

TXT records ([RFC 1035 section 3.3.14](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1035#section-3.3.14)) hold one or more character strings; the finder joins them into one value per record. A domain's TXT records live at its own name. Services that verify with a record on an underscored name use the scoping convention of [RFC 8552 section 1.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8552#section-1.1), which keeps those records out of the apex; the finder does not guess those names.

### 2. Verification tokens

A record is a token when it starts, ignoring case, with one of 35 known prefixes: `google-site-verification=`, `MS=ms`, `atlassian-domain-verification=`, `stripe-verification=`, `docusign=`, `adobe-sign-verification=`, `adobe-idp-site-verification=`, `facebook-domain-verification=`, `apple-domain-verification=`, `ZOOM_verify_`, `dropbox-domain-verification=`, `slack-domain-verification=`, `webexdomainverification.`, `cisco-ci-domain-verification=`, `hubspot-developer-verification=`, `pardot`, two GlobalSign forms, `onetrust-domain-verification=`, `have-i-been-pwned-verification=`, `citrix-verification-code=`, `smartsheet-site-validation=`, `miro-verification=`, `canva-site-verification=`, `twilio-domain-verification=`, `postman-domain-verification=`, `openai-domain-verification=`, `zoho-verification=`, `protonmail-verification=`, `yandex-verification:`, `keybase-site-verification=`, `wrike-verification=`, `logmein-verification-code=`, `amazonses:` and `mailru-verification:`. Tokens are grouped by service, so two Google tokens are one service with two tokens.

The practice these prefixes follow, publishing a random value that only the account holder knows at the domain, is described in the IETF [Domain Control Validation using DNS](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-dnsop-domain-verification-techniques/) draft, an Internet-Draft in the DNSOP working group and not an RFC when this page was written. Its recommendations (scoped names, time-bound tokens, removing the record after validation) are the background for the fixes below; the finder does not grade tokens against it.

### 3. SPF senders

A record that starts with `v=spf1` is the SPF record ([RFC 7208 section 3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-3)). Each `include:` term ([RFC 7208 section 5.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-5.2)) names another domain whose SPF record is consulted; the finder matches it, as the domain or a name under it, against 18 known senders: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, SendGrid, Mailgun, Amazon SES, Mailchimp, Mailchimp Transactional, Salesforce, Zendesk, Postmark, MailerSend, Brevo, HubSpot, Help Scout, Intercom, Fastmail, Zoho Mail and Proton Mail. It does not evaluate the record or count lookups; the [SPF Checker](https://tamperbox.dev/spf-checker) does, against the limit in [RFC 7208 section 4.6.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-4.6.4).

### 4. Unknown tokens

A record that is neither a known token nor SPF, but contains `verif`, `validation`, `site-verify`, `domain-key` or `challenge` followed by `=` or `:`, or looks like `name=value` with a value of 16 or more token characters, is listed as a likely token for a service not in the catalog. Everything else is "Other".

### 5. Findings

- Two or more tokens for one service: "N X tokens" (check). Several can be legitimate (two accounts, a re-verification), so the advice is to account for each, not to delete.
- Records that look like unknown tokens: "N unrecognised tokens" (info).
- Any known token: "Verification records are public" (info): anyone can read the list, which helps phishing that impersonates your vendors.
- No tokens and no SPF senders: "No services found" (info).

## Standards and conformance

| Specification | Sections used | How the tool applies them | Where it stops |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [RFC 1035](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1035) | [3.3.14](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1035#section-3.3.14) TXT | Character strings joined into one value per record | Records at other names are not read |
| [RFC 7208](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208), SPF | [3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-3) records, [5.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-5.2) include | `v=spf1` recognised; include targets named | Not evaluated: lookup counts, multiple-record errors ([3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7208#section-3.2)) and syntax are the SPF Checker's job |
| [RFC 8552](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8552), underscored names | [1.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8552#section-1.1) | Explains why scoped verification records are not seen at the apex | No underscored names are probed |
| [Domain Control Validation using DNS](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-dnsop-domain-verification-techniques/) (Internet-Draft) | Whole document | Background for the advice | Not a standard; tokens are not graded against it |

Each service's token format is that vendor's own convention.

## Finding reference

| Finding | Severity | What it means | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N X tokens | CHECK | More than one claim on the domain by one service; one may belong to an account you no longer control | Match each token to an account; remove the ones nobody can account for |
| N unrecognised tokens | INFO | Claims by services not in the catalog | Ask who added them before removing anything |
| Verification records are public | INFO | Anyone can list your vendors | Remove tokens once a service no longer needs them, if it allows |
| No services found | INFO | No known tokens and no SPF senders at this name | None; check CNAME or subdomain verification if you expected one |

## Guide

### Account for every token

1. List the verified domains in each service's admin console (Google Search Console and Workspace, Microsoft 365 admin center, Atlassian admin, and so on).
2. Match each token value to an account. The value is usually shown next to the domain in the console.
3. For a token nobody can account for, ask who manages DNS changes; agencies and former employees are the usual source.
4. Remove the record only when the account is confirmed unneeded. Some services re-check periodically and drop the verification when the record goes.
5. Confirm with `dig +short TXT example.com`, then check here again.

### Remove tokens after verification

Some services only need the record during verification; others keep checking. Their documentation says which. Removing the ones that allow it shortens the public vendor list.

### Review mail senders

Each SPF include lets that service send as the domain. When a service stops sending, remove its include; the [SPF Checker](https://tamperbox.dev/spf-checker) shows the lookup count and the include tree, and Take it further opens it with this record.

### Break it cases

| Case | What it loads | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A second token | Your records, without SPF, with a second token for your first service | The duplicate-token check |
| DNS records | Three `dig` answer lines | Pasted record parsing |
| Unknown tokens only | Two tokens for unknown services | The unrecognised-token reading |
| Only SPF | Your SPF record alone | Senders without tokens |
| No TXT records | `nodata.` plus your domain | The empty result |

## Worked example

The page opens on `harbor-supply.example`, answered in the browser. Its records hold `google-site-verification=` twice (one of them `0ld-ag3ncy-t0k3n-2019`), `MS=ms48213705`, and tokens for Atlassian, Stripe, DocuSign and Meta, an `acmesso-verify=` token the catalog does not know, and an SPF record with four includes.

Read record by record, the way the "Every TXT record" table shows them:

| Record | Kind | What the finder makes of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v=spf1 include:_spf.inboxcloud.example include:send.bulkpost.example include:mail.ticketdesk.example include:spf.officesuite.example ip4:198.51.100.24 ~all` | SPF | Four includes, none a known sender, so four senders not in the catalog |
| `google-site-verification=h4rb0r-5upp1y-v3r1f1c4t10n` | Token | Google: Search Console or Workspace ownership |
| `google-site-verification=0ld-ag3ncy-t0k3n-2019` | Token | A second Google token: the duplicate check |
| `MS=ms48213705` | Token | Microsoft 365 or Entra ID domain |
| `atlassian-domain-verification=…` | Token | Atlassian: Jira, Confluence or Atlassian Access |
| `stripe-verification=…` | Token | Stripe account domain |
| `docusign=…` | Token | DocuSign account domain |
| `facebook-domain-verification=…` | Token | Meta Business Manager domain |
| `acmesso-verify=…` | Unknown | Looks like a token for a service not in the catalog |

**6 services suggested on harbor-supply.example.** "2 Google tokens" is the check: the second looks like an agency's token from 2019. "1 unrecognised token" and "Verification records are public" are informational. Take it further hands the SPF record to the SPF Checker, which answers its sample includes locally. Replace the sample with your own domain and every section, card and link is rewritten for it.

## For agents

The Tamperbox MCP server has a `saas-stack` operation. Call it with the domain; it reads the TXT records at that name and its CNAME chain with its own server-side rules, returns services with token values redacted, and counts matched and unmatched records. Keep these with any conclusion:

- Verification records are service signals, not proof of current use, a subscription or a complete inventory.
- Google and Microsoft tokens do not identify a specific product.
- Never ask an agent to delete a token without confirming who owns it.

See [Connect an MCP client](/mcp/connect/) and [Security and privacy](/reference/security/).

## Limits

- One TXT lookup at one name, from one resolver at one moment.
- 35 token prefixes and 18 SPF senders; others appear as unknown or other.
- A token can be stale, copied or published by someone else with DNS access.
- Nothing is stored.
